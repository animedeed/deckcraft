# Presentation rhythm

## 1. Acts

| Act | Viewer's question | Share of the presentation | Density | Motion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | "What is this about and why should I care?" | 10% | low: title slide, statement, route | camera, motif pinwheel |
| Problem | "What's wrong right now?" | 15% | medium: hero number, example of a mistake | number appearing, document sheets |
| Method | "How do you solve it?" | 30% | high: diagrams, code, steps | wheel, pipeline, marker, loops |
| Evidence | "Does it work?" | 30% | high with breathers: charts, comparisons | bars, rings, dot field, spotlight |
| Resolution | "What follows from this?" | 15% | low: conclusion, limitations, summary | camera pull-back, assembly |

A teaching lecture has the same acts:
- "why this practical class";
- "what we submit";
- "how we do it" — step by step;
- "how to check yourself", typical mistakes;
- "what they'll ask at the defense".

## 2. Density and breathing

- **Density sawtooth.** After 2 dense slides comes a "breather": one number, one phrase or a full-screen diagram without text. Three dense slides in a row is a signal to split.
- **Scene length.** A slide in reading mode gets 40–90 s of speech, speaker mode 15–40 s. More than 2 minutes on one slide — break it into Morph steps.
- **Steps instead of a list.** Five points are shown as five states of one diagram, not as a list.

## 3. One "wow" per section

"Wow" techniques:
- a topic flower pointer beyond the screen edge;
- nesting doll;
- isometry;
- burst;
- pinwheel;
- a ribbon of document sheets growing left to right.

Rules:
- **One per section**, at its climax: the moment the method "comes together" or the result "fires".
- The other transitions are calm: persistent anchors, rearrangement, marker.
- "Wows" in two consecutive sections use different techniques.

## 4. Motion language by section

Each section gets a "vocabulary" of 2–3 techniques. That way the viewer feels the section change through motion, not through a caption.

| Section | Vocabulary |
|---|---|
| Data | dot field, document sheets, bars |
| Pipeline / method | marker, flow loop, wheel |
| Code | line highlighting, terminal cursor |
| Results | rings, re-sorting, spotlight |
| Summary | camera pull-back, rhyme with the title slide |

**Durations:**
- 0.4–0.6 s — an edit (marker; changed text — fade);
- 0.7–0.9 s — rearrangement;
- up to 1.2 s — camera flight (default profile);
- service half-steps — 0.4–0.6 s.

**Native transitions** (`cube`, `gallery`, `curtains`) — only at act boundaries, 1–3 kinds per presentation.

## 5. Persistent anchors and rhyme

- **Anchors:** the "03 / 18" footer, progress dots, section color, the motif, the `!!panel` backing that changes shape. They hold the feeling of a single film even when headers change.
- **Rhyme:** the first and last slides share one world or motif, with the result highlighted in the finale. Within a section, the first and last slides are one diagram in different states: "before → after".
